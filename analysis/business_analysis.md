# CRM Pipeline Analysis

## Scope and caveat

This report is generated by `build_analysis.py` from the exported marts. It uses the
synthetic CRM dataset as of 2026-09-01. Findings are descriptive operational
observations, not causal claims or a forecast. Lead-source, product, and team effects
are planted simulation parameters, so they demonstrate the analysis workflow and are
not real-world evidence.

## Findings

### 1. Most open pipeline is older than any deal has ever taken to close

The longest closed sales cycle in the data is 180 days. Yet
378 of 590 open opportunities (64.1%) are
older than that, representing $7,375,050. 242 of them are
not flagged by the 30-day stale rule because they still log activity, so activity-based
staleness alone misses them. These deals need a close-out decision.

### 2. Stale follow-up should be value-prioritized

183 open opportunities have had no activity for more than 30 days,
worth $3,756,350 (33.3% of open pipeline value).
The queue is ranked by the heuristic `amount * days_since_activity` (not a recovery
probability). Top five:

- Rodriguez LLC (Michele Williams): $201,800, 292 days since activity
- Wilkerson-Day (Gina Moore): $110,800, 381 days since activity
- Robinson-Brock (Derek Zuniga): $80,100, 437 days since activity
- Diaz Inc (Allison Hill): $68,300, 428 days since activity
- Baker and Sons (Dylan Miller): $54,800, 499 days since activity

### 3. Rep results need volume context

Allison Hill has the largest open pipeline at $944,900
(66.7% win rate on 12 closed deals). Among reps with at least
10 closed deals, win rates range from 31.3% (Ryan Munoz, n=16) to
75.0% (Cristian Santos, n=12). With samples this small, treat the
spread as a review signal, not a performance ranking.

### 4. Opportunity creation peaked in May 2026

63 opportunities were created that month. One synthetic period does not
establish seasonality.

### 5. Win-rate segments (95% Wilson intervals)

- Lead source: Referral wins 67.3% (53.4-78.8%, n=49) versus
  ZoomInfo List 22.2% (13.2-34.9%, n=54). The intervals
  do not overlap, so the gap is larger than sampling noise (in this simulation).
- Product: Platform License 54.9% versus Support Add-on 40.7%. The intervals
  overlap, so this gap is not statistically distinguishable at this sample size.
- Opportunities with no lead source win only 35.7% (n=70) and make up
  171 of 900 records, so missing lead source is also a data-quality issue.

### 6. Stage progression to date

83.4% of opportunities reached Qualification, 76.6% of those reached Proposal,
and 71.5% of those reached Negotiation. From Negotiation, 35.5% closed won and
39.9% closed lost. These are progressed-to-date rates: open deals that have not moved yet
count as not progressed, so later-stage rates are understated until those deals resolve.

## Recommended operating actions

1. Run a weekly close-out review of the 378 opportunities older than 180 days, highest value first.
2. Work the stale queue top-down, and require a status confirmation before counting any of it as recoverable.
3. Review reps with closed-deal volume, pipeline mix, and deal age together. Do not rank on win rate alone.
4. Make lead source a required field. Resolve the 34 amount issues and 39 missing DUNS records through CRM hygiene workflows.
5. Report pipeline with and without imputed amounts ($11,277,500 vs $10,964,300).
